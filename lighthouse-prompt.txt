Build a single self-contained HTML file: an animated pixel-art cutaway
(cross-section) of a 19th-century lighthouse on a rock at sea. It runs on its
own through a continuous day/night cycle, like a living diagram.

HARD CONSTRAINTS
- One file, index.html, with inline CSS and JS. No network requests of any
  kind: no libraries, CDNs, web fonts, images, or audio. All art is drawn
  procedurally in code (Canvas 2D or WebGL).
- Works by opening the file from disk in current Chrome, Firefox, and Safari.
  No build step. No console errors.
- Render to a low-resolution internal buffer (about 480x270) and scale it up by
  whole-number factors with nearest-neighbour sampling so pixels stay crisp.
  Letterbox to fit any window size and handle resize.
- Pixel-art discipline: a small, deliberate palette. Shading and light come
  from palette shifts, banding, and dithering, not smooth alpha gradients or
  blur.
- Animate with requestAnimationFrame and delta time. Simulation speed must not
  depend on frame rate. Target 60 fps on a mid-range laptop.
- Deterministic: use a seeded PRNG, never Math.random. The scene's state must
  be a function of simulated time, so that jumping to any time of day shows the
  correct state everywhere (keeper position, drive weight height, lamp on or
  off, sky).
- URL parameters: ?t=HH:MM starts at that simulated time, ?paused=1 starts
  paused, ?seed=N sets the seed.

THE SCENE
- The tower is shown in cutaway: the near wall is removed to reveal the
  interior, with the wall's thickness visible at the cut edges and exterior
  masonry still visible at the sides.
- Four floors, bottom to top:
  1. Oil store: casks, oil cans, shelves.
  2. Keeper's quarters: bunk, stove with a fire, table with logbook, pendulum
     clock.
  3. Watch room: weight-driven clockwork with meshing gears, and a drive weight
     on a chain that hangs down through the floors below.
  4. Lantern room: a Fresnel lens rotating around a lamp, glazing, a gallery
     with a railing, a domed copper roof with a vent and a weathervane.
- A spiral stair connects all floors.
- Outside: a rock base with breaking waves, sea to the horizon, a distant
  headland, sky with stars, moon, sun, and clouds, and a keeper's cottage
  beside the tower with lit windows and chimney smoke.

ANIMATION (the mechanisms must be coupled, not independent loops)
- Gears: at least four, with visible teeth. Adjacent gears turn in opposite
  directions at speeds inversely proportional to their tooth counts.
- Drive weight: descends at a rate tied to the gear train. When it nears the
  bottom, the keeper winds it back up.
- Lens: its rotation is driven by the same gear train. Because the view is
  side-on, the beam should lengthen, shorten, and flash as it sweeps past the
  viewer, and it should light whatever it crosses (sea, clouds, rock).
- Pendulum clock: swings steadily, and its hands show the simulated time.
- Keeper: a small character with a daily routine. He walks and climbs the
  stair along its actual geometry (no teleporting), checks the oil, winds the
  clockwork, lights the lamp at dusk, puts it out at dawn, writes the log, and
  sleeps.
- Day/night: a full 24 hours passes in about 3 minutes. The sky palette moves
  through dawn, day, dusk, and night. Stars fade in and out, the sun and moon
  travel, and warm interior light (stove, lamp, windows) dominates at night.
- Weather: a wind value that drifts over time and drives the weathervane, the
  chimney smoke, cloud speed, and wave height together.
- Sea: animated waves, foam on the rocks, and reflections of the moon and the
  beam.

OVERLAY UI (use a bitmap pixel font defined in the file)
- Title: invent a name, location, and founding year for the lighthouse.
- A numbered callout for each floor and for the stair, with a leader line to
  the room and a one-line subtitle naming its contents.
- Status panel: simulated time, wind, lamp state, and lens rotation rate. Every
  value is read live from the simulation.
- Keeper's log: one or two lines describing what the keeper is doing right
  now, changing as his activity changes.
- Timeline scrubber: drag to set the time of day. The whole scene responds
  consistently.
- Keys: space pauses, left and right arrows change simulation speed.
